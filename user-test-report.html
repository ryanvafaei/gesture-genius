1 · Where session time went

Each verbose session drawn on a shared clock. Calibration and menus take more time than the exercises themselves.

▼ marks a skipped exercise. Grey = the safety screen after Stop. Session 008 and 010 calibration includes an arm exercise that was never finished.

What worked
  • The hand-exercise loop (intro → calibrate → reps → summary) ran end to end in 11 of 12 sessions. No verbose log shows a crash; each one closes with quit or goodbye.
  • Thumb bend 9/9 and memory pairs 4/4 reps succeeded; bubble pinch 17/23, finger spread 11/15.
  • Calibration passed on the first try in 12 of 14 hand calibrations. In 31 of 59 movement reps people went past their calibrated maximum.
  • Difficulty adapted: both “not good” check-ins (004, 013) started at target 0.56 instead of 0.70, and targets moved within 13 of 18 blocks (range 0.50–0.73).
  • Tracking was stable once an exercise ran: the affected hand was visible in 80–99% of exercise frames in 6 of 7 sessions, at about 28–30 fps (p95 frame loop ≤ 44 ms).
  • The safety path fired instantly in all 3 Stop presses (004, 006, 009) with the 112 advice; 009 resumed after 11 s.
  • Every exercise opened with a daily-life purpose (“holding a cup of tea”) and summaries named it (“Today you practised for reading, cooking…”).
What did not work
  • Arm exercises: 0 of 4 attempts finished. Three were skipped in intro or calibration, one was locked (“for when your therapist is with you”). In 010 the coach said “Please turn your chair so your right arm is nearest the screen” 8 times in 55 s.
  • Sequence tasks rarely passed: finger tapping 1 of 3 runs, thumb-to-finger 0 of 1, and 3 more runs were skipped. A run with 7 of 8 correct lifts still counted as a failed rep.
  • Overhead dominates: calibration (28%) and menus/greeting (28%) outweigh exercising (21%).
  • 98 tracking alerts in 7.8 min of exercise; “wrong hand” (51) often flipped on and off within 0.1 s. Cloth-squeeze calibration gave a near-zero range (closure 0.883→0.900) and one squeeze block was skipped after the hand kept dropping out.
  • The default volume was too long: “Short sessions” was switched on in 7 of 8 verbose sessions. Without it, 008 got 2×8 reps and skipped set 2.
  • Every guest was greeted “Hello Eleanor”, and Stop notes say “She pressed Stop”.
  • End ratings reached 4 of 12 people; the garden reward never grew past stage 0. The gesture menu build (012) ended after 52 s with 0 reps.

3 · Hints and compensation vs success

Rep success rate by how many corrective hints the coach gave during the rep, and whether compensation was flagged.

Wrist movement made up 12 of the 15 compensation flags. Most common prompts before failed reps: “Hold it a little longer” and “Try to keep your wrist still”.

Strengths

  • Personal calibration per exercise makes targets fit each hand, and people met or exceeded them.
  • Check-in feeds the plan: a “not good” answer lowers targets and marks the day as difficult.
  • Warm, consistent coaching voice: about 14 utterances per minute with counting, praise and form cues.
  • Logging is rich: per-rep range, hold stability, hints, compensation, plus frame-level landmarks and video.
  • Safety Stop is always reachable and responds immediately.

Weaknesses

  • Too much time before and between reps; a first-time user waits about 1.5 min for the first rep.
  • Hand identity is fragile when both hands, an object (cloth) or a side view are in frame.
  • All-or-nothing scoring on sequence tasks hides near-misses (7/8 = fail).
  • Arm exercises cannot be completed in this seated, single-camera setup.
  • Some logs contradict themselves: 006 is tagged “TargetRaised” while both its targets went down (0.70→0.53, 0.70→0.55). The generated data/report stops at 12:15 and misses 011–013.

What we learned

  1. A first session is mostly setup. The value per minute depends more on calibration and navigation than on the exercises.
  2. Reach is not the bottleneck; holding still for the full hold time is. A rep with one coach hint succeeds 57% of the time against 90% without.
  3. Tracking breaks at transitions: after calibration, when both hands or an object are in view, and in side-on arm poses.
  4. How people feel matters. The two “not good” check-ins produced a Stop (004) and the only low enjoyment score (013: 1/5).
  5. Participants experienced effort as high (mean 4.0 of 5) even in ~5-minute sessions.
  6. In the 8 verbose sessions every exercise was picked with a number key; no pick by gesture or voice was logged.

Suggested improvements

  • Cut setup time: reuse the last calibration and offer re-measuring, shorten intros for repeat exercises, and default first-time users to short sessions.
  • Steadier hand tracking: lock onto the calibrated hand, smooth handedness over ~0.5 s before raising an alert, and treat a held object as expected in the squeeze task.
  • Fairer scoring: partial credit for sequence tasks and a visible hold timer; shorten the hold after a hint instead of failing the rep.
  • Arm exercises: start with the affected arm, stop repeating a prompt after two tries and offer skip, or remove them from unsupervised use.
  • Guest mode: greet without a name, use neutral wording in notes, and always ask the three end ratings, also after Stop or quit.
  • Better logs: record who pressed each key, the reason for Stop, and regenerate the report after the last session.

Gaps in this evidence

  • 12 people, one session each: no data on learning, progression or the garden over time.
  • Verbose logs missing for 002, 003, 011 and 013; end ratings from only 4 people.
  • No participant profile. Affected hand is “Left” for all 13 guests (default), so we cannot tell if testers matched the target group.
  • No ground truth: rep success and tracking accuracy are the system’s own judgement; video was not annotated.
  • Mixed builds: 012 used a different (gesture) menu, so its result is not comparable.
  • No usability scale (SUS/UEQ) or interview notes in the data.
